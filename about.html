Our story

One borrowed lane, seventeen years ago.

Tidewater began because a coastal city full of water had almost nowhere safe to learn to swim.

2009 → today

Built by coaches, one lane at a time.

Grace Mwangi started Tidewater with eight children, one rented lane and a whistle borrowed from a school. She had spent a decade coaching age-group swimmers and kept meeting the same problem: families living twenty minutes from the Indian Ocean whose children could not swim.

Seventeen years later we run six lanes of our own, a full squad program and an adult school that teaches roughly two hundred nervous beginners a year. The method has not changed — small groups, clear milestones, and coaches who stay long enough to know every swimmer by name.

We are not trying to produce champions. We are trying to make sure nobody on this coast is afraid of water — and then, if they want it, we make them fast.

Grace Mwangi — Founder & Head Coach

Mission & values

Water safety first, performance second, always in that order.

●

Safety is non-negotiable

A qualified lifeguard is on deck for every session, group sizes are capped, and coaches re-certify in lifesaving annually.

●

Progress you can see

Every level has written milestones and a six-week review, so families always know exactly what the swimmer is working on.

●

Open to everyone

Twelve funded places each term go to local school pupils who would otherwise never get lane time.
